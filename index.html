04 // Definition: AI (Artificial Intelligence)

Artificial: Made by human hands and tools—not occurring naturally on its own.

Intelligence: The ability to take in new information, learn from observations, adapt, and solve problems to reach a goal.

A Big Lesson: Humans are still required! AI does not have common sense, empathy, or human intuition—that deep gut feeling where a person spots subtle context, fairness, or creative leaps that raw math misses. It needs human directors, coders, and judges to steer it in the right direction.

05 // The Real Cost of AI
Financial Costs
  • Personal: Free tier tools vs. $20/month premium subscriptions for high-tier models.
  • Company Level: Billions of dollars spent on specialized AI superchips (GPUs) and massive engineering teams.
  • Global Scale: Hundreds of billions poured into data centers, electrical grids, and undersea cables.
Environmental Footprint & Water Needs
  • AI Water Consumption: Data centers consume billions of liters of potable fresh water directly for evaporative cooling towers. Generating 10 to 50 AI prompts can evaporate up to 500ml (a full bottle) of fresh water.
  • Global Water Comparison: Today over 2 billion people lack safely managed drinking water at home. AI data centers are competing directly with local agricultural irrigation and municipal drinking water in drought-stressed regions.
  • Electricity Demand: A single AI query uses roughly 5x to 10x more energy than a traditional web search.
  • Hardware E-Waste: Server clusters and high-power GPUs wear out or become obsolete in just 3 to 5 years, producing massive electronic waste.
Hyperscale Data Center ↗ Row upon row of high-power server racks running 24/7.
GPU Supercomputers ↗ Clusters of ultra-fast graphics processors powering AI brains.
Direct Liquid Cooling ↗ Coolant connectors piping fluids directly across boiling-hot AI chips.
Chilled Water Loops ↗ Industrial pumps and radiators dissipating massive thermal loads.
Hardware E-Waste ↗ Piles of discarded server boards replaced when newer chips arrive.
06 // Foundation: Statistics

Predicting outcomes using historical observations: How do you predict who wins a soccer match? Who runs faster? You don't guess blindly—you examine past matches, weather conditions, stamina, injuries, and speed records. What factors do you consider when coming up with your answer? That is how AI models calculate predictions from data.
